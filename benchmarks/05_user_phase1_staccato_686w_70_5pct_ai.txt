Extended Abstract: Chemo-Mechanical Degradation Dynamics and Interfacial Stability in All-Solid-State Lithium-Metal Batteries: Multi-Scale Continuum Modeling and Operando Synchrotron Diagnostics

Solid-state lithium-metal batteries transform modern electrochemical energy storage. They yield high energy densities and strong safety profiles. Traditional liquid-electrolyte lithium-ion cells fall short. Global transit and grid sectors demand higher storage capacity. Rapid charging damages solid-state cells during cycling. Chemo-mechanical degradation limits commercial growth. Metallic lithium anodes react with solid inorganic electrolytes. Solid-electrolyte interphase layers undergo unstable evolution. Continuous galvanostatic cycling creates microscopic voids. Contact loss follows stripping. Lithium dendrites propagate across buried boundaries. Electrochemical performance drops. Structural integrity fails. Local current density spikes at rough contacts. Stress concentrations accelerate catastrophic short circuits. Interfacial decay caps lifespan. Rate capability collapses under high loads. Multi-scale chemo-mechanical dynamics operate from atomistic to continuum domains.

Solid-electrolyte interfaces present complex chemo-mechanical physics. Coupled phenomena span disparate spatial and temporal scales. Standard transport models parameterize ion flux through Butler-Volmer kinetics. These classic formulations assume planar electrode boundaries. They work for macro-scale thermodynamic behaviors. Yet they ignore micro-scale surface roughness. Localized stress builds up without tracking. Non-uniform electrodeposition kinetics remain unmeasured. Ceramic electrolyte matrices suffer mechanical fracture under load. Intergranular cracks propagate along boundaries. Anisotropic grain boundary conductivity drives current hotspots. Degradation compounds under high current density. We built a coupled elastoplastic phase-field framework. The model integrates vacancy diffusion with interfacial stress. Electrochemical reaction kinetics complete the system. We map void nucleation during lithium stripping. Dendrite penetration occurs during plating. Our chemical potential function incorporates elastic strain energy. It tracks surface tension and electrostatic overpotential. The model defines stable operating boundaries. It suppresses morphological instability in ceramic cells.

Multi-modal operando characterizations validate our continuum theoretical model. Experiments evaluated symmetrical and full-cell configurations. Dense ceramic lithium lanthanum zirconium tantalum oxide pellets formed symmetrical cells. High-temperature spark plasma sintering densified the pellets. High-resolution operando synchrotron X-ray computed tomography tracked morphological changes. Beamline imaging captured three-dimensional evolution at buried interfaces. Operando Raman spectroscopy monitored chemical species evolution. Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry tracked interphasial composition dynamics. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy measured interfacial resistance growth. We evaluated three interfacial engineering strategies. These included atomic layer deposition of aluminum oxide interlayers. In-situ chemical processing formed lithiophilic metal alloy interphases. Mechanical pressure modulation spanned 0.5 to 7.0 megapascals. Custom test fixtures housed integrated load cells to record stack pressure.

Multi-scale diagnostics reveal clear degradation mechanisms. Unengineered lithium-garnet symmetrical cells showed fast interfacial impedance escalation. Area-specific resistance grew by 312% over fifty cycles. Tests ran at 1.0 milliampere per square centimeter. Synchrotron tomographic reconstructions isolated the root cause. Extensive void coalescence caused early failure during stripping. Active electrochemical contact area shrank by 43.8%. A five-nanometer atomic-layer-deposited aluminum oxide buffer suppressed void nucleation. The layer stabilized interfacial charge transfer. Engineered cells retained an area-specific resistance of 18.4 ohm-square centimeters after three hundred cycles. This marks an 82.6% reduction in resistance growth. Operando chemical mapping confirmed smooth current distribution across metal alloy interphases. The alloy layer raised the critical current density threshold. Dendrite initiation jumped from 1.2 to 3.8 milliamperes per square centimeter.

Laboratory diagnostics guide industrial solid-state cell design. Nano-engineered interlayers stabilize electrochemical charge transfer. Yet surface coatings cannot replace mechanical pressure management. Unoptimized stack pressure drives interfacial creep. High pressure cracks brittle solid electrolyte separators. Manufacturing consortia need standardized operando testing protocols. Facilities must track impedance drift and thermal fluctuations under dynamic driving cycles. Grain boundary passivation and stoichiometric dopant optimization improve durability. Compliant interlayers absorb volume changes during cycling. Electric vehicle manufacturers need these robust design principles. Chemists must collaborate with mechanical modeling engineers. Characterization specialists must join industrial cell producers. Joint work scales defect-free solid-state laminates.

Solid-state lithium-metal batteries offer high energy density. Uncontrolled chemo-mechanical degradation limits interfacial stability. Clean energy systems require high storage capacity and fast charging. The field balances energy density with long-term stability. Our continuum mathematical framework links directly to synchrotron operando diagnostics. The model predicts interface evolution under aggressive cycling. Future work will test self-healing composite polymer-ceramic electrolytes. Flexible matrix designs accommodate volume changes during deep discharge. Commercial solid-state batteries depend on atomistic interface design. Continuum mechanical optimization and scalable manufacturing complete the system.
