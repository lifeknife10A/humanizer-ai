Interfacial contact loss destroys solid-state lithium cells during early cycling. Metallic lithium anodes offer a theoretical capacity of 3,860 mAh g⁻¹ alongside an ultra-low redox potential. In coin cells, active cycling ruins interfacial contact within fifty cycles. Under 1.0 mA cm⁻² current density, initial coulombic efficiency drops to 86.4%. Capacity retention plunges below 60% after forty cycles. Parasitic chemical reactions consume active lithium at the electrolyte face. These side reactions form resistive interphases containing LiOH, Li₂CO₃, and porous Li₂O. Rapid charge cycles accelerate this degradation. During stripping, lithium atoms evacuate the interface faster than bulk metal can deform. Microscopic voids nucleate across the contact plane. Active contact area shrinks progressively under current drain. During plating, metallic filaments pierce ceramic grain boundaries. Electrical short circuits follow. Localized current density spikes and mechanical stress concentrations degrade the cell. Peak currents compound damage. Interfacial decay caps practical cycle life and rate capability. To track these failure modes, we couple continuum elastoplastic modeling with operando synchrotron diagnostics. Tomography maps buried void growth in real time.

Our diagnostic measurements reveal clear degradation pathways governing cell lifespan. Baseline unengineered lithium-garnet symmetrical cells exhibited rapid interfacial impedance escalation during initial cycling. Area-specific resistance jumped 312% across the first fifty cycles at a current density of 1.0 mA cm⁻². Cell resistance skyrocketed quickly. Coatings prevented dendrite shorts.

Cyclic mechanical loads degrade solid-state cells over extended cycling. External stack pressures maintain interfacial contact between soft lithium foil and rigid ceramic electrolytes. Unchecked loads accelerate interfacial creep. In our load frames, LLZTO pellets cracked whenever static pressure exceeded 6.5 MPa. Mechanical loads compound stress. Conversely, pressures below 1.0 MPa allowed voids to expand along contact planes. Dynamic fixtures held stack pressure steady across cycling. Depositing thin lithium fluoride interlayers passivated ceramic grain boundaries. Stoichiometric dopant tuning further stabilized garnet microstructures. This dropped interfacial resistance by orders of magnitude. Soft alloy buffers absorbed mechanical strain. Roll-to-roll tape casting produced thirty-micrometer LLZTO ceramic membranes with a fracture toughness of 1.1 MPa m^{1/2}. Calendering LLZTO sheets under continuum stress control yielded crack-free, pouch-scale laminates.
