Extended Abstract — Chemo-Mechanical Degradation Dynamics and Interfacial Stability in All-Solid-State Lithium-Metal Batteries: Multi-Scale Continuum Modeling and Operando Synchrotron Diagnostics

Solid-state lithium-metal batteries promise superior energy density and improved safety over conventional liquid-electrolyte configurations. Rapid electrification across transport and grid sectors drives the demand for high-capacity chemistries. Commercial adoption stalls. Interfacial degradation during rapid charge-discharge cycling causes early cell failure. Unstable solid-electrolyte interphases form between metallic lithium anodes and solid inorganic electrolytes. During prolonged galvanostatic cycling, microscopic voids and lithium dendrites degrade cell integrity. Contact loss accelerates failure. Local current density variations and mechanical stress concentrations trigger catastrophic short circuits. Interfacial decay caps performance. This work combines continuum electrochemical modeling with operando synchrotron diagnostics to map these degradation pathways.

Interfacial physics in solid-state batteries spans coupled chemo-mechanical processes across multiple scales. Standard models parameterize ion flux via Butler-Volmer kinetics assuming planar boundaries. Macro-scale transport fits these assumptions. Yet classical theory ignores microscopic surface roughness and non-uniform electrodeposition. Solid electrolyte matrices remain vulnerable to mechanical fracture and anisotropic grain boundary conduction. High current densities compound damage. To resolve these limitations, we formulate a coupled elastoplastic phase-field model integrating vacancy diffusion and interfacial stress evolution. The math reflects physics. The framework captures void nucleation during stripping and dendrite growth during plating. Elastic strain energy and electrostatic overpotential define the generalized chemical potential. Microscopic stresses drive failure. This formulation establishes precise operating boundaries to suppress morphological instabilities.

We validated the continuum model using a multi-modal operando characterization campaign on symmetrical and full-cell architectures. Direct imaging captures growth. Symmetrical lithium-garnet cells contained dense ceramic lithium lanthanum zirconium tantalum oxide pellets prepared by spark plasma sintering. Synchrotron X-ray computed tomography tracked three-dimensional morphological evolution at the buried interface during active cycling. Interfacial impedance rose steadily. Operando Raman spectroscopy and secondary ion mass spectrometry tracked chemical species evolution and composition dynamics. We evaluated surface coatings alongside mechanical pressure control from 0.5 to 7.0 MPa. Stack pressure dictates contact. Atomic layer deposition produced ultra-thin aluminum oxide interlayers, while chemical synthesis formed lithiophilic metal alloy interphases. Load cells logged stack pressure continuously.

Diagnostic measurements reveal clear degradation pathways governing cell lifespan. Unmodified lithium-garnet symmetrical cells suffered rapid impedance growth, with area-specific resistance jumping 312% across the first fifty cycles at 1.0 mA cm⁻². Cell resistance skyrocketed quickly. Synchrotron tomography confirmed that void coalescence during stripping caused failure, cutting active electrochemical contact area by up to 43.8%. Voids severed contact paths. Depositing a five-nanometer aluminum oxide buffer layer suppressed void nucleation and stabilized charge transfer. Cells with this interlayer maintained an area-specific resistance of 18.4 Ω·cm² after three hundred cycles, cutting resistance growth by 82.6% compared to baseline cells. The coating preserved contact. Operando chemical mapping showed that conformal metal alloy interphases homogenized local current distribution, raising the critical current density threshold for dendrites from 1.2 to 3.8 mA cm⁻².

These findings offer design principles for commercial solid-state battery manufacturing. Stack pressure dictates stability. Nano-engineered interlayers stabilize electrochemistry, yet require external pressure management. Unoptimized stack pressures accelerate interfacial creep and fracture brittle ceramic separators. Thermal shifts compound stress. Cell makers must establish standardized operando protocols to track impedance drift under dynamic loads. Durability requires multi-scale control. Combining grain boundary passivation with compliant interlayer design protects commercial cell architectures. Cross-disciplinary teams drive scaling. Industrial cell producers must integrate modeling with material synthesis to produce defect-free, large-area solid-state laminates.

This study outlines the chemo-mechanical constraints governing solid-state lithium-metal batteries. Fast charging compounds degradation. Expanding clean energy storage requires balancing high energy density against long-term interfacial stability. Models must match experiment. Linking continuum modeling to synchrotron operando diagnostics provides a predictive framework to stabilize solid-electrolyte interfaces under aggressive cycling regimes. Volume changes strain solids. Future work will investigate self-healing polymer-ceramic composites to manage expansion during deep discharge. Commercial success demands synergy. Commercial adoption hinges on aligning interfacial chemistry with scalable cell manufacturing.
