Interfacial contact loss destroys solid-state lithium cells during early galvanostatic cycling. Lithium-metal anodes achieve theoretical capacities of 3,860 mAh g⁻¹, but practical lifetimes remain brief. During active cycling, contact loss ruins performance. Unstable interphases grow between lithium and ceramic separators. Fast charging accelerates this decay. Stripping leaves microscopic voids across the buried interface. Plating then forces lithium filaments through ceramic grain boundaries. Short circuits follow. Local current density spikes and mechanical stress concentrations degrade the cell. Interfacial decay caps lifespan. We pair continuum modeling with operando synchrotron diagnostics. These tools isolate and suppress buried failure modes.

Chemo-mechanical stresses couple across microscopic interfaces. Butler-Volmer kinetics conventionally predicts ion flux across ideal planar boundaries. Macro-scale transport fits these assumptions. Classical theory ignores surface roughness and non-uniform electrodeposition. Solid electrolyte matrices fracture under localized mechanical stress. High current densities compound damage. The model captures void nucleation during stripping and dendrite growth during plating. Elastic strain energy and electrostatic overpotential define generalized chemical potential. Microscopic stresses drive failure. This formulation establishes operating boundaries to suppress morphological instabilities.

Our diagnostic measurements reveal clear degradation pathways governing cell lifespan. Unmodified lithium-garnet cells suffered rapid impedance growth during initial cycling. Area-specific resistance jumped 312% across the first fifty cycles at 1.0 mA cm⁻². Cell resistance skyrocketed quickly. Synchrotron tomography confirmed that void coalescence during stripping caused failure. This cut active electrochemical contact area by up to 43.8%. Voids severed contact paths. Depositing a five-nanometer aluminum oxide buffer layer suppressed void nucleation and stabilized charge transfer. Cells with this interlayer maintained an area-specific resistance of 18.4 Ω·cm² after three hundred cycles. This reduced resistance growth by 82.6% compared to baseline cells. The coating preserved contact. Operando chemical mapping showed that conformal metal alloy interphases homogenized local current distribution. Critical dendrite current densities expanded from 1.2 to 3.8 mA cm⁻².
