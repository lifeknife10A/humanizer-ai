# Mathematical & Theoretical Foundations of AI Text Detection

This document formalizes the information-theoretic, probabilistic, and statistical mechanics governing modern AI text detectors (including ZeroGPT, Turnitin, GPTZero, and CopyLeaks), explaining why standard LLM outputs are flagged and how deterministic syntactic transformations manipulate classifier confidence.

---

## 1. Information-Theoretic Framing

Autoregressive Large Language Models (LLMs) generate text token-by-token by sampling from a conditional probability distribution parameterized by neural weights $\theta$:

$$P_\theta(W) = \prod_{i=1}^N P_\theta(w_i \mid w_1, w_2, \dots, w_{i-1})$$

Text detectors evaluate whether a given sequence $W = (w_1, \dots, w_N)$ was generated by a human author or sampled from an autoregressive distribution. Detectors do not possess semantic understanding; they measure **statistical predictability** and **structural variance** against an evaluation language model $\mathcal{M}_{\text{eval}}$ (typically a masked language model like RoBERTa or an autoregressive model like GPT-2 XL).

---

## 2. Perplexity (Cross-Entropy Loss)

Perplexity measures how "surprised" a language model is by a sequence of words. It is monotonically related to the cross-entropy loss $\mathcal{H}(W)$:

$$\mathcal{H}(W) = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \log_2 P_{\mathcal{M}}(w_i \mid w_{<i})$$

$$\text{PPL}(W) = 2^{\mathcal{H}(W)} = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \ln P_{\mathcal{M}}(w_i \mid w_{<i})\right)$$

### The Low-Perplexity Attractor
During inference, decoding strategies (greedy decoding, top-$k$, top-$p$ nucleus sampling with $p \in [0.8, 0.95]$, or low temperature $\tau \le 0.7$) force the model to select tokens from the highest probability mass:

$$w_i \sim \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{z_i}{\tau}\right)$$

Consequently:
- **AI-generated prose** consistently exhibits low cross-entropy ($\mathcal{H} < 2.5$ nats, $\text{PPL} < 15$). Every word is predictable.
- **Human academic prose** exhibits frequent unpredictable word choices, idiosyncratic terminology, domain-specific constants, and abrupt transitions ($\mathcal{H} > 4.5$ nats, $\text{PPL} > 45$).

### Sliding-Window Perplexity
Detectors do not merely compute a global document perplexity. They compute a local sliding-window perplexity $\text{PPL}_k(t)$ across window size $k$ (typically $k \in [15, 30]$ tokens):

$$\text{PPL}_k(t) = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \ln P_{\mathcal{M}}(w_{t-j} \mid w_{<t-j})\right)$$

If $\text{PPL}_k(t) < \tau_{\text{threshold}}$ over a sustained run of tokens, the entire span is colored **yellow / flagged** as synthetic.

## 3. Burstiness (Variance of Perplexity and Length)

While perplexity measures average predictability, **burstiness** measures the non-uniformity and variance of that predictability across the text.

### Perplexity Burstiness
Let $S = (s_1, s_2, \dots, s_M)$ be the sentences of document $W$. The sentence perplexities $P_j = \text{PPL}(s_j)$ have sample mean $\mu_P$ and sample standard deviation $\sigma_P$:

$$\mu_P = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M P_j, \quad \sigma_P = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M-1}\sum_{j=1}^M (P_j - \mu_P)^2}$$

The perplexity burstiness coefficient $B_P$ is defined as the coefficient of variation:

$$B_P = \frac{\sigma_P}{\mu_P}$$

### Sentence Length Burstiness
Similarly, let $L_j = |s_j|$ denote the word count of sentence $s_j$. The length variance $V_L$ and length burstiness $B_L$ are:

$$\mu_L = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M L_j, \quad \sigma_L = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M-1}\sum_{j=1}^M (L_j - \mu_L)^2}, \quad B_L = \frac{\sigma_L}{\mu_L}$$

### The Failure of the "Staccato" Uniformity
When an LLM is instructed to "write short sentences", it produces uniform staccato:
- Sentence 1: 7 words
- Sentence 2: 8 words
- Sentence 3: 7 words
- Sentence 4: 8 words
- Sentence 5: 6 words

Here:
$$\mu_L \approx 7.2, \quad \sigma_L \approx 0.8 \implies B_L = \frac{0.8}{7.2} \approx 0.11$$

A burstiness coefficient $B_L < 0.25$ indicates **machine synthesis**.
Natural human writing exhibits high variance: a 16-word explanatory compound, followed by a 4-word micro-punch, followed by an inverted 12-word clause:
- Sentence 1: 16 words
- Sentence 2: 4 words
- Sentence 3: 13 words
- Sentence 4: 5 words
- Sentence 5: 18 words

$$\mu_L \approx 11.2, \quad \sigma_L \approx 6.1 \implies B_L = \frac{6.1}{11.2} \approx 0.54$$

High burstiness ($B_L > 0.45$, $B_P > 0.50$) destroys the detector's classifier confidence because natural human cognition oscillates between deep explanation and punchy emphasis.

## 4. Zipf's Law and High-Entropy Domain Anchors

Zipf's law states that the frequency $f$ of any word is inversely proportional to its rank $r$ in the frequency table:

$$f(r) \propto \frac{1}{r^s}, \quad s \approx 1$$

In academic AI text, models disproportionately pull from the top rank-frequency tier of academic discourse:
- Discourse markers: *Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, Consequently, Notably, Ultimately*
- Prestige abstractions: *landscape, pivotal, tapestry, cornerstone, paramount, delve, testament*
- Vague conclusions: *balanced synergy, transformative potential, collaborative coordination*

### High-Entropy Perturbation
When abstract generalizations are replaced with **exact empirical physical parameters**, the local token entropy spikes sharply:

$$H(w_t) = -\sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}} P(v \mid w_{<t}) \log_2 P(v \mid w_{<t})$$

Replacing *"high-temperature sintering"* with:
$$\text{spark plasma sintering at } 1150^\circ\text{C under } 50\text{ MPa uniaxial pressure for } 15\text{ minutes}$$

And replacing *"lithium garnet separator"* with:
$$\text{dense ceramic } \text{Li}_{6.4}\text{La}_3\text{Zr}_{1.4}\text{Ta}_{0.6}\text{O}_{12} \text{ (LLZTO) with } 98\% \text{ relative density}$$

Injects low-frequency tokens ($r > 50,000$ in standard corpora) that evaluate with high surprisal in general language models, forcing local window perplexity above the detection threshold without losing academic rigor.

## 5. Syntactic Structure & Grammar Tree Entropy

Modern detectors trained on transformer representations (e.g. RoBERTa classifiers) learn syntactic tree embeddings. Standard LLM text is dominated by canonical Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) structures:

$$\text{S} \to \text{NP} + \text{VP} \to \text{NP} + \text{V} + \text{NP}$$

When every sentence starts with a noun phrase subject:
1. *Solid-state lithium batteries deliver...*
2. *Metallic lithium anodes offer...*
3. *Microscopic voids nucleate...*
4. *Mechanical stress concentrates...*

The transition probability between sentence boundaries and noun phrases approaches unity:

$$P(\text{POS}_1 = \text{NOUN} \mid \text{EOS}) \approx 0.95$$

### Prepositional and Adverbial Inversion
By forcing clause inversions:
$$\text{S} \to \text{PP} + \text{,} + \text{NP} + \text{VP}$$
$$\text{S} \to \text{AdvP} + \text{,} + \text{NP} + \text{VP}$$

Examples:
- *Under 1.0 mA cm⁻² current density, initial coulombic efficiency drops...*
- *At solid-solid boundaries, mechanical stress couples directly...*
- *During stripping, lithium atoms evacuate...*
- *In our load frames, LLZTO pellets cracked...*

The positional entropy $H(\text{POS}_1 \mid \text{EOS})$ increases dramatically:

$$H(\text{POS}_1 \mid \text{EOS}) = -\sum_{t \in \{\text{NOUN}, \text{PREP}, \text{ADV}, \text{VERB}, \dots\}} P(t \mid \text{EOS}) \log_2 P(t \mid \text{EOS})$$

This disrupts the syntactic fingerprint that neural classifiers use to distinguish machine generation from human writing.

## 6. The Triad Attractor ("Rule of Three")

In autoregressive generation, enumerative phrases frequently converge on three elements ($A, B, \text{ and } C$).
Why? Because the conditional probability of predicting the conjunction `"and"` peaks sharply after the second comma:

$$P(\text{token} = \text{"and"} \mid w_{t-1} = \text{","}, \text{count}(\text{comma}) = 2) \gg 0.70$$

Detectors treat symmetrical 3-element lists as high-confidence $n$-gram indicators. 

### Deterministic Triad Collapse
The deterministic transformation:
$$\text{Triad}(A, B, C) \to A \text{ and } B$$
Collapses the trigram pattern, breaking the statistical detector's expected $n$-gram sequence and eliminating one of the highest-weight synthetic features.
